TenVoices: Speech Recognition
from Ten Voices

Which of 50 phrases was spoken — by a voice the system has never heard?

Tanvir Ahmed · Md. Shahriar Rakib Rabbi · Mahir Ahmed · Shafil Ahmed

1
THE TASK

Recognise one of 50 phrases, from unfamiliar speakers

  • Input: one spoken command or sentence (“turn the lights on”).
  • Output: which of the 50 phrases it was, with a confidence.
  • The hard part: the test speakers are never heard in training.

RESEARCH QUESTIONS

  • Which approach recognises speech most accurately?
  • Does longer fine-tuning help?
  • How do new speakers, accents and noise affect results?
2
DATA

Two published 10-speaker subsets

TENVOICES-FSC · MAIN

From Fluent Speech Commands (Lugosch et al., 2019): 10 speakers (5 F, 5 M) × 50 smart-home commands × 2 takes = 1,000 clips.

Many commands share most of their words: “switch the lights on” vs “off”.

TENVOICES-L2 · ACCENTS

From L2-ARCTIC (Zhao et al., 2018): 10 non-native speakers — first languages Hindi, Mandarin, Spanish, Korean, Vietnamese × 50 sentences = 500 clips.

Selected by script with fixed rules; audio never redistributed.

3
METHOD

Four stages, one shared pipeline

All audio: mono, 16 kHz, silence trimmed, peak-normalised, padded to 6 s.

1 · MFCC + CLASSIC ML

13 MFCCs + Δ + ΔΔ, mean and std (78 values) → k-NN, SVM, Random Forest, XGBoost.

2 · UNTRAINED BASELINE

wav2vec 2.0 with a new, untrained head: the chance level.

3 · FINE-TUNED

wav2vec 2.0 for 3 / 8 / 15 epochs, white-noise augmentation; also WavLM, HuBERT and a frozen linear probe.

4 · WHISPER

Zero-shot speech-to-text; the transcript is matched to the nearest of the 50 phrases.

4
EVALUATION

Fair comparison: seen vs unseen speakers

RANDOM SPLIT

80 / 20, stratified by phrase. The test speakers were also heard in training.

SPEAKER-INDEPENDENT · HEADLINE

5 folds: 7 training, 1 validation, 2 test speakers; every speaker is tested once.

  • Accuracy, macro F1, confusion matrices, accuracy per speaker; word error rate for Whisper.
  • Best epoch and hyper-parameters chosen on validation data, never on the test set.
  • White noise added to the test clips at 20 / 10 / 5 dB.

10
CONCLUSIONS

What we learned

  • Self-supervised pre-training makes 50 commands learnable from 7 training speakers.
  • MFCC models depend heavily on the speaker and fail in noise.
  • Noise augmentation trades a little clean accuracy for robustness.
  • Zero-shot Whisper is the strongest and most portable, but accents raise its word errors.

FUTURE WORK

  • Fine-tune Whisper or wav2vec 2.0 (CTC) on non-native speech.
  • Multilingual pre-training (XLS-R).
  • Bangladeshi speakers reading the same commands.
  • All 97 FSC speakers: how accuracy scales with speakers.
